Pixel VFX Anatomy

A generator for code-drawn pixel effects, built from a study of how PixelFX draws its art. Every seed produces a different effect, either a one-shot like a strike or a seamless loop like an aura or portal. Its shape, material, timing and palette all come from that one number. No image files. Each frame is computed at 160 × 96 and scaled up without smoothing.

How a seed becomes an effect

    

      

        
One seed, one recipe

        

          
A seed starts a seeded random sequence. The draws pick a shape and a material (flame, frost, storm, dust, void, poison, holy, water), then set about 25 numbers for that shape. There are twenty-five one-shot shapes (strike, slam, nova, eruption, shatter, slash, beam, pillar, implosion, spikes, firework, wave, chain, claw, meteor, quake, dash, starburst, warp, and the UI pops pickup, level up and hit number, and the screen transitions wipe, iris and dissolve) and twenty-two loops (aura, portal, vortex, sigil, brazier, orb, ward, missile, fountain, cloud, beacon, swarm, breath, ladder, core, pool, scan, cascade, and the critters bats, slime, ghost and moths), so 47 shapes × 8 materials makes 376 types, each with its own range of variations. A second sequence from the same seed runs the particles, so the same seed always plays the same frames.

          
Seeds come in generations, so adding shapes never changes an old link. Seeds up to 2,147,483,647 are the first generation: nine shapes and five materials. Up to 4,294,967,295 is the second: sixteen shapes and eight materials. Up to 8,589,934,591 is the third: 27 shapes. Up to 12,884,901,887 is the fourth: 37 shapes. Up to 17,179,869,183 is the fifth, which draws from all 47. Each plays exactly as it always has. Generate picks from the newest.

        

      

      

        
Seamless loopsTry: Generate → Loops

        

          
One-shots simulate particles step by step. Loops work the other way: every frame is computed from the time alone, wrapped at the period. Each moving part runs a whole number of cycles per period (a mote orbits once or twice, a flame tongue rises and is reborn), so the last frame flows into the first with no seam. Depth still matters: orbiting motes pass behind the figure on the far side of their orbit, and floor rings draw their far half first.

Generated palettesTry: Keep this palette

        

          
Each recipe builds its own eight-step ramp from a random base hue. Lightness climbs from near black to near white. Saturation peaks in the middle and drops at the top so highlights read as white-hot. The hue drifts as it climbs, with highlights leaning toward yellow and shadows leaning the other way. That drift is what keeps a generated ramp from looking like a plain gradient.

          

          

        

      

      

        
Guard rails

        

          
Random numbers alone make noise, so every recipe keeps the parts the study showed matter: an anticipation phase, a white hero frame on contact, a burst of light that falls off, a spread of particles, and a long decay that ends with an empty frame. Ranges are clamped so nothing leaves the canvas or overwhelms the focal point. Some seeds are still plainer than others.

        

      

    

  


  

    
Compared with PixelFX

    
The generator trades curation for range. PixelFX is a library of finished effects that someone looked at and refined. This page makes new effects without limit, at a lower and less even level of polish.

The drawing techniques underneath

    

      

        
Two buffersTry: View buffer

        
Each frame has a color buffer of hard pixels and a light buffer of additive glow, added on top at the end. The color layer stays crisp. All the softness and heat comes from light. "Color only" looks flat. "Light only" shows how much of the picture is glow.

      

      

        
Dither, not alphaTry: Partial alpha

        

          
A half-transparent pixel is drawn or skipped by comparing its alpha to a 4 × 4 Bayer threshold for that screen position, so fades break into a clean pattern instead of muddy in-between colors. Smoke, vapour and dust are the exception: they are truly translucent soft discs.

          

            
            
alpha 0top: dithered · bottom: blended1

          

        

      

      

        
Lines and light, not fills

        
Shapes are 1 px rims, rings, streaks and sparks. Cores and leading edges are white-hot, middles are tinted, and the deepest color only shows in shadow.

      

      

        
Particles with physicsTry: ¼× speed

        
Sparks are two-tone lines that stretch with speed, fall and bounce. Flames rise, cool down the ramp and turn to smoke. Shards spin, bounce and settle. Lights flare on contact and fall off with life². The simulation steps at a fixed 120 Hz, so drag the scrubber back and the same frame returns.

      

      

        
Choreography and hit-stopTry: Hit-stop off

        
Anticipation, a white hero frame, release, spread, hold and a long decay. On contact everything freezes for a few hundredths of a second while the screen shakes. Without the freeze, the same effect feels weightless.

      

      

        
Other looksTry: Look → Cel outline, Handheld, Ink sketch

        
The same buffers can be finished another way. In the cel look, anything at least a third covered becomes a solid shape with a one-pixel ink outline, and glow is cut into three flat bands instead of falling off smoothly. Smoke and vapour become solid outlined puffs. The handheld look maps every pixel's brightness onto four shades of one green, dithering between them. The ink sketch draws on paper: outlines round every shape, cross-hatching that thickens as colours darken so white-hot cores stay bare, and glow as a light hatch that fades to stipple. Nothing about the effect changes, only how its last step is drawn.

      

      

        
Depth with flat tricks

        
Floor rings are flattened ellipses: the far half draws behind and dimmer, the near half in front. Cracks and scorch sit a few pixels below the floor line as perspective.
